The regression front-end

Calibrated forecast features for online regression. Preliminary, July 2026.

Replace each input stream with two scalars from its own Laplace forecaster — the predictive mean and the standardized surprise — and give the target its own pair, leaving the target itself raw. The regression then learns from variables that are stationary in scale, bounded in influence and calibrated. The same substitution has already been measured for anomaly detectors and for forecasters.

The ablation

Taking the pair apart: z alone is useless, because surprises carry no level. The mean alone costs a lot on clean data and misses distortion. Together they recombine into a conditional mean that neither supports on its own.

The strongest result came from a condition the first pass missed. LaplaceTarget alone, with raw features untouched, beats river's pipeline on three of their four datasets clean (TrumpApproval 0.301 vs 0.334) and 10/10 under feature spikes, and ties the counterexample. So: add the target pair always; replace features with their pairs only when you distrust the features; never use z alone.

Two further comparisons. Raw target lags, the obvious alternative, are strong on smooth single-entity series and fragile elsewhere; the wrapper on top of lags improved the best configuration found on three of four datasets. An EWMA version of the pair, which costs nothing, captures much of the protection and wins the smoothest dataset outright, but loses by 14-26% wherever the stream has structure it cannot track.

The theorem

Regret transfer through the front-end. Pathwise, with measured constants. The sandwich's log-loss regret against every conjugated linear-Gaussian refinement of the body is bounded by an explicit O(d log T) on any data sequence at all — no stationarity, moment or calibration assumption needed, because the clamp supplies the boundedness the online-learning theorem requires. A corollary at s = 1 and u = 0: the sandwich can never lose more than O(d log T) nats to the forecaster alone. That places the output-sandwich pathology seen in the study in point extraction rather than in the density. Proofs and a numerical check of the bound are in the repo.

Where it stops working

Are five calibrated numbers usually enough? On 126 non-price FRED cross-series pairs, across three learners: no. There the signal is the raw contemporaneous level of a correlated sibling, and the (mean, surprise) pair loses part of the level — rebuilding it needs the product of surprise and scale, which a linear learner cannot form. The pair sits about 30% behind raw features on clean data; the contamination protection narrows that, and including the raw value alongside the pair recovers most of the gap. Two learner notes from the same study: river's default SGD linear model silently diverges on tiny-scale streams, while its BayesianLinearRegression handles them and was used for the verdict.

Summary

Forecast features added on top of raw lags are worth nothing; the protection comes from substitution, not addition. On plain linear universes, send raw lags. Use the calibrated features when you distrust the channel, when the stream has structure a lag cannot express, or when you should be forecasting rather than regressing.

Status

Preliminary. Still open: per-entity bodies for entity-interleaved streams, multi-horizon surprises from k=3 bodies, and concept-drift classification. Reproduction is five resumable harnesses in the timemachines repo with fixed seeds.
