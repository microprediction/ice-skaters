Live demo

A stream, two models, and a fat-finger button. Everything on this page runs in your browser: the forecasters are the skaters JavaScript twin, and the baseline is a faithful port of river's recommended pipeline.

The stream is a simple driven process: a hidden signal x pushes the target y around. The two models receive, each tick: baseline, thirteen raw numbers, the current x, four lags of x, and eight lags of y; fronted, exactly four numbers, what its x-forecaster expected x to be and how surprising the actual x was, plus the same expectation-and-surprise pair for y from its y-forecaster. No raw values reach the fronted regression; its two forecasters hold all the history. That is generous to the baseline, which gets more raw history than the forecasters exploit. Both predict one step ahead, learn, and move on. Press the buttons to corrupt a reading, at the benchmark study's six-to-ten-sigma scale, and watch. The baseline standardizes with a running mean and deviation, so a bad tick arrives at full force and poisons the statistics; the fronted model receives what its forecasters expected and a surprise capped near seven. On clean data the baseline is slightly ahead, and that is the toll the papers page reports; corruption is where the lanes diverge.
