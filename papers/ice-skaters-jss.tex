\title{Skaters on a River: Calibrated Forecast Features for Streaming Regression, with a Pathwise Regret-Transfer Theorem}

\Plaintitle{Skaters on a River: Calibrated Forecast Features for Streaming Regression}
\Shorttitle{\pkg{ice-skaters}: Calibrated Forecast Features}

\newcommand{\iceabstract}{
The \proglang{Python} package \pkg{ice-skaters} replaces each numeric stream
entering a streaming machine-learning pipeline with two scalars produced by
the stream's own online distributional forecaster: the one-step predictive
mean, which carries the level, and the standardized surprise, the probability
integral transform of the arriving value pushed through the standard normal
quantile, which carries the news and is bounded near $|z| = 7$ by
construction. The package ships two estimators compatible with the
\pkg{river} streaming-learning protocol: a transformer for input streams and
a regressor wrapper that adds the target's own pair while leaving the target
raw. On \pkg{river}'s own regression datasets under \pkg{river}'s
progressive-validation protocol, the wrapper alone beats the recommended
\code{StandardScaler} pipeline on three of four datasets untouched and ten
seeds out of ten under simulated feature contamination, while tying the
fourth; in controlled simulation the full substitution beats raw features, a
running z-score, a median/MAD winsorizer, and a Huberised loss thirty seeds
out of thirty under every contamination type tested, at a small clean-data
toll. We report the losing rows with the winning ones: distance-based
learners do not benefit, entity-interleaved streams handicap a single
forecaster per key, informative heavy tails should not be tamed, and the
front-end costs roughly $900\times$ a running scaler per tick. The
theoretical contribution is a pathwise regret-transfer theorem with measured
constants and no calibration assumption: conjugated through the forecaster's
predictive CDF, an online linear refinement of the surprises achieves
log-loss within an explicitly computable $O(d \log T)$ of every refinement in
its class, on any data sequence whatsoever, and in particular can never lose
more than $O(d \log T)$ nats to the forecaster alone.
}

\section{Introduction} \label{sec:intro}

A streaming regression consumes raw numbers it has no right to trust. The
standard remedy in streaming libraries is a running affine standardization: a
\code{StandardScaler} for the features and, in \pkg{river}
\citep{montiel2021river}, a \code{TargetStandardScaler} for the target. This
paper measures, and then partially proves, the value of a stronger
preprocessing contract: give every numeric stream its own online
\emph{distributional} forecaster, and hand the learner the forecaster's state
instead of the raw value.

Concretely, each stream contributes two scalars per tick. The first is the
one-step predictive mean, what the forecaster expected this value to be. The
second is the standardized surprise
\begin{equation}
  \bar z_t \;=\; \Phi^{-1}\!\big(\mathrm{clip}(\hat F_t(y_t),\,
  \varepsilon,\, 1-\varepsilon)\big), \qquad \varepsilon = 10^{-12},
  \label{eq:surprise}
\end{equation}
the probability integral transform \citep{rosenblatt1952} of the arriving
value under the predictive CDF $\hat F_t$ issued for it, pushed through the
standard normal quantile and clamped so $|\bar z_t| \le \zmax =
\Phi^{-1}(1-\varepsilon) \approx 7.03$. The mean carries the level; the
surprise carries the news. A wild observation can move the pair only so far,
and that bounded influence is where the robustness comes from. When the
forecaster is calibrated the surprises are approximately independent standard
normal, so the learner receives variables that are stationary in scale and
carry, by construction, only what the forecaster could not explain.

The idea is the regression instance of a front-end thesis established
elsewhere for anomaly detectors and for forecasters: other methods improve
when run in the coordinates of a calibrated online forecaster
\citep{cotton2026skaters}. The forecaster used throughout is \code{laplace}
from the \pkg{skaters} package, a likelihood-weighted ensemble over
conjugated transform chains, though nothing in the construction or the
theorem depends on that choice.

The paper makes three contributions. First, a package: \pkg{ice-skaters}
ships \code{LaplaceFeatures}, a \pkg{river} transformer for input streams,
and \code{LaplaceTarget}, a regressor wrapper that adds the target's own
pair, both pipe-, pickle- and deep-copy-compatible with the \pkg{river}
estimator protocol (Section~\ref{sec:package}). Second, evidence: a
three-part benchmark study, contaminated simulation, \pkg{river}'s learners
against \pkg{river}'s recommended pipeline, and \pkg{river}'s own datasets,
followed by an ablation that decomposes the pair and reverses part of the
initial recommendation (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}). Third, a theorem:
pathwise regret transfer with measured constants and no calibration
assumption anywhere (Section~\ref{sec:theory}), whose insurance corollary
resolves the one catastrophic failure mode the study observed.

\section[The package]{The package} \label{sec:package}

Installation is \code{pip install ice-skaters}; dependencies are
\pkg{skaters} and \pkg{river} only. The full recipe reads:

\begin{verbatim}
from river import linear_model, preprocessing
from ice_skaters import LaplaceFeatures, LaplaceTarget

model = LaplaceTarget(
    regressor=preprocessing.TargetStandardScaler(
        regressor=LaplaceFeatures()
        | preprocessing.StandardScaler()
        | linear_model.LinearRegression()))
\end{verbatim}

\code{LaplaceFeatures} maintains one forecaster per numeric feature key and
replaces the value of key \code{k} with \code{mu\_k} and \code{z\_k} as
above. Non-numeric values pass through untouched. Non-finite numeric values
are imputed by the forecast itself, the predictive mean with $z = 0$
(``expected value, no news''), and do not advance the forecaster; a running
scaler, by contrast, propagates NaN. \code{LaplaceTarget} wraps any regressor
in the style of \code{TargetStandardScaler} and augments the features with
the target's own pair, which a transformer cannot supply because it never
sees the target; the target itself is never transformed.

Three implementation contracts matter in practice. \emph{Alignment}:
\pkg{river}'s \code{Pipeline.learn\_one} updates unsupervised transformers
before transforming for downstream steps, which would hand the model fresher
features at learn time than it predicted with; \code{LaplaceFeatures} caches
the pre-update feature dictionary during \code{learn\_one} and serves it to
the immediately following \code{transform\_one}, so the predict and learn
paths see identical features and each forecaster advances exactly once per
sample. \emph{Serialization}: instances hold only plain state, never
closures, so both estimators pickle and deep-copy. \emph{Robustness}:
observations are winsorized before a forecaster consumes them, at $10^{60}$
absolute plus a window twelve orders of magnitude above the current
predictive level; the window is magnitude-relative rather than
sigma-relative, because after a degenerate-variance stretch (missing-data
zeros, say) a legitimate value sits billions of sigmas out and must pass. The
gate is exact identity on any stream double arithmetic can represent
comfortably.

The measured cost is about $390$ microseconds per stream per sample
single-threaded, roughly $900\times$ \pkg{river}'s \code{StandardScaler}, or
about $2{,}500$ samples per second per stream. That is the price of a full
distributional forecaster per stream; it is right for polls, sensors, and
market bars, and wrong inside a hot path at hundreds of thousands of ticks
per second.

\subsection{The ablation, and the revised recommendation}

Decomposing the pair on the simulation: the surprise alone is useless (excess
MSE 17 to 146; surprises carry no level, and a level cannot be regressed from
pure news), the mean alone pays a large clean toll and misses distortion, and
together they recombine into a conditional mean neither scalar supports
alone. On \pkg{river}'s datasets the sharpest cell is the one the first pass
missed and Table~\ref{tab:river} reports: \code{LaplaceTarget} alone, raw
features untouched, beats the pipeline on three of four datasets untouched,
wins ten seeds of ten under $2\%$ feature spikes on those three despite its
features being the raw, spiked ones, and ties the counterexample. The target
pair anchors the prediction and reduces reliance on corrupted features.

The recommendation therefore orders itself: add the target pair always, one
wrapper, helps clean and contaminated alike; replace input features with
their pairs only when the features themselves are distrusted; never use the
surprise alone. A cheap-rollup control, an EWMA version of the same pair,
captures the tail insurance for free and even wins the smoothest dataset as
features, while losing by 14--26\% wherever the stream has structure an EWMA
cannot track: the rollup shape is free insurance, the calibrated forecaster
is the accuracy upgrade, and on near-random-walk series the strongest object
remains the forecast alone. A fairness audit added the referee's baseline, the same
pipeline plus raw target lags $1..8$: on the smooth single-entity series raw
lags are strong and spike-resistant and the wrapper alone loses to them, but
the wrapper on top of lags is best of all, clean and spiked, and everywhere
else the wrapper beats the lag ladder outright, since a lag ladder inherits
every pathology of the raw stream. Raw lags and forecaster features are two
ways to buy the same history; lags are the fragile way.

\section{Theory: pathwise regret transfer} \label{sec:theory}

The empirical study contains one catastrophic cell: learning against the
target's own surprise and mapping point predictions back through a
contaminated forecaster amplified target spikes by $60\times$. The theorem
below explains both why the density version of that construction is
nevertheless safe on any data whatsoever, and why the front-end's boundedness
buys worst-case guarantees no raw-coordinate method has. Throughout, nothing
is assumed about the data or about forecast quality; the reader may imagine
an adversary choosing $y_{1:T}$.

\subsection{Setup}

A \emph{body} is any causal algorithm that, having seen $y_1, \dots,
y_{t-1}$, emits a predictive distribution for $y_t$ with continuous, strictly
increasing CDF $\hat F_t$ and strictly positive density $\hat f_t$; Gaussian
mixtures, hence every skater, qualify. The unclamped surprise is $z_t =
\Phi^{-1}(\hat F_t(y_t))$ and the clamped surprise $\bar z_t$ is
Equation~\ref{eq:surprise}. Let $\phi_t \in \R^d$ be any causal features with
$\|\phi_t\|_\infty \le \zmax$, for instance a constant together with clamped
surprises of exogenous streams and of the target's own past, which is what
the package emits.

\begin{lemma}[Conjugation identity] \label{lem:conj}
For any density $q$ on $\R$,
\[
p_q(y) \;=\; \hat f_t(y)\, \frac{q(z_t(y))}{\varphi(z_t(y))},
\qquad z_t(y) = \Phi^{-1}(\hat F_t(y)),
\]
is a probability density, and $\log p_q(y_t) = \log \hat f_t(y_t) + \log
q(z_t) - \log \varphi(z_t)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substituting $z = z_t(y)$, whose derivative is $dz/dy = \hat
f_t(y)/\varphi(z)$, gives $\int p_q = \int q = 1$; the identity is the
definition evaluated at $y_t$.
\end{proof}

The identity is pathwise and exact for the actual, prequentially estimated
$\hat F_t$ \citep{dawid1984}, however miscalibrated; forecast quality never
enters. The refinement class is Gaussian, $q_m = \mathcal N(m, s^2)$ for
fixed $s > 0$. The \emph{sandwich} uses $m_t = \hat z_t$, the prediction of
the forward (Vovk--Azoury--Warmuth) forecaster
\citep{vovk2001,azoury2001,cesabianchi2006} trained on the clamped surprises:
with $A_t = \lambda I + \sum_{s \le t} \phi_s \phi_s'$ and $b_{t-1} =
\sum_{s < t} \bar z_s \phi_s$, it predicts $\hat z_t = \phi_t' A_t^{-1}
b_{t-1}$. Note $A_t$ includes the current $\phi_t$; that forward-looking
regularization is the Azoury--Warmuth device.

\subsection{The theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Pathwise regret transfer] \label{thm:main}
For every sequence $y_{1:T}$, every comparator $u \in \R^d$, and with $C =
\{t : \hat F_t(y_t) \notin [\varepsilon, 1-\varepsilon]\}$ the set of
clamp-active ticks,
\[
\sum_{t=1}^T \Big[\log p_{q_{u'\phi_t}}(y_t) - \log p_{q_{\hat z_t}}(y_t)\Big]
\;\le\; \frac{1}{2s^2}\Big[\lambda \|u\|^2 + \zmax^2 \,
\ln \frac{\det A_T}{\lambda^d}\Big]
\;+\; \frac{1}{s^2}\sum_{t \in C} (u'\phi_t - \hat z_t)(z_t - \bar z_t),
\]
and $\ln(\det A_T/\lambda^d) \le d\ln(1 + T\zmax^2/\lambda)$ under the
feature bound. Every quantity on the right-hand side is computable from the
run: $\lambda$, $s$ and $\zmax$ are chosen, $\det A_T$ and the
clamp-correction sum are observed, and $C$ consists of ticks the body
assigned probability below $10^{-12}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:conj}, for any two refinement means $a, b$ the log-score
difference at tick $t$ is $[(z_t - b)^2 - (z_t - a)^2]/2s^2$, so the
left-hand side equals $\tfrac{1}{2s^2}\sum_t [(z_t - \hat z_t)^2 - (z_t -
u'\phi_t)^2]$. Since $g(z) = (z - \hat z_t)^2 - (z - u'\phi_t)^2$ is affine
in $z$ with slope $2(u'\phi_t - \hat z_t)$,
\[
(z_t - \hat z_t)^2 - (z_t - u'\phi_t)^2 = (\bar z_t - \hat z_t)^2 - (\bar z_t
- u'\phi_t)^2 + 2(u'\phi_t - \hat z_t)(z_t - \bar z_t),
\]
and the correction vanishes off $C$ where $z_t = \bar z_t$. The clamped sum
is the squared-loss regret of the forward forecaster on targets bounded by
$\zmax$, which Appendix~\ref{app:vaw} bounds by $\lambda\|u\|^2 + \zmax^2
\ln(\det A_T/\lambda^d)$. The determinant bound is
$\det A_T \le (\mathrm{tr}\, A_T/d)^d$ with $\mathrm{tr}\,A_T \le \lambda d +
T d \zmax^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Insurance] \label{cor:insurance}
Take $s = 1$ and $u = 0$. Then $q_0 = \varphi$ and $p_{q_0} = \hat f_t$ is
the body itself, so for every data sequence
\[
\sum_t \log \hat f_t(y_t) - \sum_t \log p_{q_{\hat z_t}}(y_t)
\;\le\; \frac{\zmax^2}{2}\, d \ln\!\Big(1 + \frac{T \zmax^2}{\lambda}\Big)
+ \sum_{t\in C} 2\,|\hat z_t|\,|z_t - \bar z_t|.
\]
The sandwich can never lose more than $O(d \log T)$ nats to the body,
pathwise, while gaining without limit whatever linear structure exists in the
surprises.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
The corollary resolves the study's catastrophic cell. The output sandwich
looked disastrous there, but that failure was a property of extracting a
point forecast, the mean of the pushforward, whose tails a contaminated body
inflates. As a density, scored where densities are scored, the sandwich is
safe on any data by Corollary~\ref{cor:insurance}. Fix the inputs for point
prediction; trust the sandwich only in log-loss.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The oracle decomposition}

Only now suppose a truth: $(x_t, y_t)$ adapted to a filtration $\mathcal
G_t$, with $P_t$ the conditional law of $y_t$ given $\mathcal G_{t-1}$, and
let the body be causal in $y$ alone with $\mathcal F_{t-1} =
\sigma(y_{1:t-1})$. Let $Z_t^*$ and $\bar Z_t$ denote the conditional laws of
$z_t$ given $\mathcal G_{t-1}$ and $\mathcal F_{t-1}$ respectively.

\begin{proposition}[Transfer entropy plus measured miscalibration]
\label{prop:decomp}
\[
\E \sum_t \KL(P_t \,\|\, \hat F_t) \;=\; \sum_t I\big(y_t;\, \mathcal G_{t-1}
\mid \mathcal F_{t-1}\big) \;+\; \E \sum_t \KL\big(\bar Z_t \,\|\, \mathcal
N(0,1)\big).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
KL divergence is invariant under an invertible transformation applied to both
arguments; applying $y \mapsto \Phi^{-1}(\hat F_t(y))$, which is $\mathcal
F_{t-1}$-measurable and strictly increasing, maps $P_t$ to $Z_t^*$ and $\hat
F_t$ to $\mathcal N(0,1)$, so $\KL(P_t \| \hat F_t) = \KL(Z_t^* \| \mathcal
N(0,1))$. The chain rule of relative entropy against a fixed reference
splits, in expectation over $\mathcal G_{t-1}$, $\E\,\KL(Z_t^* \| \mathcal
N(0,1)) = \E\,\KL(Z_t^* \| \bar Z_t) + \E\,\KL(\bar Z_t \| \mathcal N(0,1))$,
and the first term is the conditional mutual information after the same
invariance is applied once more.
\end{proof}

The body's expected excess over the full-information oracle splits exactly
into the transfer entropy of the exogenous information and the body's own
prequential miscalibration, measured as the divergence of the actual
conditional surprise law from standard normal. The refinement learner of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} competes for the first term only; the second is the
body's business, and it is a measured quantity, never an assumption.

\subsection{What is not claimed, and the numerical check}

No claim that the surprises are independent, standard normal, or stationary.
No claim about point predictions on the original scale;
Section~\ref{sec:evidence} measured where those go wrong. The comparator
class is the conjugated linear-Gaussian family, not all forecasters; its
force is that it contains the body itself and every linear refinement of its
surprises, with constants a run can print. A test in the package repository
runs the exact construction, Laplace bodies, clamped surprises, forward
learner, on clean and spiked streams and asserts the theorem's inequality for
a grid of comparators; the bound becomes informative (smaller than the
trivial total loss) near $T \approx 2{,}500$ for $d = 3$.

\section{Related work} \label{sec:related}

Reversible instance normalization and its relatives
\citep{kim2021reversible,passalis2019dain} remove per-instance mean and
variance around a deep forecaster and add them back: the affine,
window-based special case of the present construction, welded to a specific
model class. The nonparanormal \citep{liu2009nonparanormal} estimates
monotone marginal transforms so that a Gaussian model fits in transformed
space; the front-end is its dynamic analogue, with conditional, time-varying
margins supplied by a forecaster rather than unconditional ranks. Robust
streaming regression modifies the loss \citep{huber1964}; Table~\ref{tab:sim}
argues the coordinates are the better lever, since a clipped residual cannot
repair a corrupted feature. The regret machinery is the forward algorithm of
\citet{vovk2001} and \citet{azoury2001} as presented by
\citet{cesabianchi2006}; the contribution here is the transfer through the
conjugation and the observation that the parade clamp manufactures the
boundedness those results assume, turning an assumption into a property.

\section{Limitations and next steps} \label{sec:limits}

The boundaries are stated in Section~\ref{sec:evidence}: distance-based
learners, entity-interleaved streams, informative heavy tails, and the
$900\times$ per-tick cost. A broadened sufficiency study on 126 non-price
FRED cross-series pairs, run across three learners, adds the outer boundary
with its mechanism: the signal there is the raw contemporaneous level of a
correlated sibling, and the (mean, surprise) pair is a lossy encoding of a
level, since rebuilding one requires the product of surprise and predictive
scale that a linear learner cannot form. Against river's own
BayesianLinearRegression on raw features, the rollup sits roughly $30\%$
behind untouched, the contamination insurance narrows the gap, and including
the raw value alongside the pair recovers most of it. The recipe for
same-tick covariates is therefore raw level plus pair; the pair earns its
keep on history-shaped channels, contaminated channels, and as the target
wrapper. Two learner facts travelled with the study: river's default SGD
linear model silently diverges on tiny-scale streams, while its
BayesianLinearRegression handles them well. An additive companion study
closed the map: the forecast features added on top of raw lags are worth
nothing on that universe (the target forecast adds a median $0.16\%$ over
the raw lag), and the contamination insurance turns out to be a property of
substitution, never of addition. The verdict in one sentence: on plain
linear universes send raw lags; the calibrated features earn their place
when a channel is distrusted (substitute, for insurance), when a stream has
structure a lag cannot express (represent), or when regression has no job
at all (forecast). Open items, in order: per-entity bodies with
partial pooling in surprise space (the \code{ChickWeights} fix);
multi-horizon surprises from $k > 1$ parades for drift and shift; and
concept-drift classification. The package and all harnesses are open source;
every table in this paper regenerates from fixed seeds.

\section{The forward-algorithm bound} \label{app:vaw}

\begin{proposition}[\citealp{vovk2001,azoury2001}; \citealp{cesabianchi2006},
Theorem 11.8]
For targets $|\bar z_t| \le Y$ and predictions $\hat z_t = \phi_t' A_t^{-1}
b_{t-1}$ as above,
\[
\sum_t (\hat z_t - \bar z_t)^2 - \sum_t (u'\phi_t - \bar z_t)^2 \;\le\;
\lambda\|u\|^2 + Y^2 \ln \frac{\det A_T}{\lambda^d} \qquad \text{for all } u.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Let $w_t = A_t^{-1} b_t$ and define the potential $\Phi_t = \min_w
[\lambda\|w\|^2 + \sum_{s\le t}(w'\phi_s - \bar z_s)^2]$, attained at $w_t$.
Per-tick algebra expresses $(\hat z_t - \bar z_t)^2$ as $(\Phi_t -
\Phi_{t-1})$ plus an extra term that telescopes against $\bar z_t^2\,
\phi_t'A_t^{-1}\phi_t$; summing,
\[
\sum_t (\hat z_t - \bar z_t)^2 \;\le\; \Phi_T + \sum_t \bar z_t^2\, \phi_t'
A_t^{-1} \phi_t \;\le\; \lambda\|u\|^2 + \sum_t (u'\phi_t - \bar z_t)^2 + Y^2
\sum_t \phi_t' A_t^{-1} \phi_t.
\]
Finally $\phi_t' A_t^{-1} \phi_t = 1 - \det A_{t-1}/\det A_t \le \ln(\det
A_t/\det A_{t-1})$, using $1 - 1/r \le \ln r$, and the sum telescopes to
$\ln(\det A_T/\lambda^d)$.
\end{proof}
